## 音频原文 (Transcript)

```text
Narration: three students are discussing which student club to join next semester.

Speaker1: Hey, I’ve been thinking about joining a club next semester, but I’m not really sure which one to choose. My coursework is fairly heavy, so I don’t want to waste my spare time on something meaningless. Do you guys have any suggestions on what I should join? I want something interesting, but also something that’s actually worth my time.

Speaker2: Honestly, I think it really depends on your interests. You should choose something you genuinely enjoy, otherwise it’s hard to stay committed especially when schoolwork gets busy. And you know, if you can’t find a club that fits you, you could even start your own club. That way, you can shape it exactly the way you want.

Speaker3: Actually, I joined the debate club last year, and it’s been a great experience. But I have to say, running a club—or even being actively involved—does require quite a lot of organization. There are usually different roles, like a president, a vice president, event coordinators, and people responsible for promotion and logistics. Everyone has their own responsibilities to keep things running smoothly.

Speaker1: Start my own club? That sounds a bit challenging. I’ve never really thought about that before. I already have plenty of school assignments to deal with, so taking charge of a whole new club would probably burn me out.

Speaker2: Exactly. That’s why starting your own club can be rewarding, but it also comes with a lot of responsibility. You need people to manage different aspects, and it takes time to build everything from scratch. Recruiting members and preparing activities can take up lots of your free hours.

Speaker3: Yeah, in the debate club, we also have regular training sessions, internal competitions, and even external tournaments. Some people focus on preparing arguments, while others help organize events or manage the team. It’s quite structured, and that’s why it works so well. I can tell how much effort goes into keeping all these activities going.

Speaker1: Hmm, hearing all that, I think starting my own club might be too much for me. I’m not really prepared to take on that kind of responsibility. I guess I’d rather just join an existing club instead of creating one from scratch. I hope I can find one that balances fun and personal growth.

Speaker2: That’s totally fine. Joining an established club is definitely easier, and you can still gain a lot from it without the pressure of managing everything. You can make new friends and pick up practical skills at the same time.

Speaker3: Yeah, and you can always try a few different clubs at the beginning of the semester. Most clubs have trial sessions, so you can see what suits you best before committing. That gives you a chance to feel the atmosphere without making long‑term promises.
```
