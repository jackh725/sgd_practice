## 音频原文 (Transcript)

```text
Narration: three students are discussing how to keep active at university.

Speaker1: I’ve spent so much time studying that I barely have energy for exercise. Besides, I want to stay active on campus, but club membership fees and parking costs are really high. I’m looking for low‑cost ways to keep fit and social. Do you two have any practical ideas?

Speaker2: That’s a great question. I really enjoy sports. I run and work out with fitness equipment daily, and I prefer swimming pools over open water. Our university actually provides lots of free student‑only facilities, such as the campus swimming pool. And sometimes, the simplest choice is just going for a walk around campus. I think these activities are relaxing and budget-friendly choices that don't put pressure on your finances.

Speaker3: I get it. My schoolwork is also really heavy this semester. I like watching Olympic games, yet swimming does not suit me. I find gym workouts too repetitive, so I would recommend walking as a cheap and easy way to relax. Sometimes I also read in libraries or cafes to unwind.

Speaker1: I’m not keen on intense outdoor sports either, but I love walking in the park or around the playground. Breathing fresh grass and enjoying sunshine calms me down, and it is much cheaper than going to the gym. I also like doing simple stretches. After your suggestions, I’m thinking about trying our Olympic‑standard campus swimming pool.

Speaker2: That is a fantastic choice. Exercising right after classes can be really relaxing. If you go swimming, you can bring your own snacks or use student discounts at the campus café for a coffee afterwards. It makes the whole experience nicer without spending much money.

Speaker3: Walking is still my favourite option. It needs no special equipment and fits any busy study schedule. Short walks in green areas clear my mind. Apart from walking, cooking simple meals at home also helps me recover energy from heavy assignments.

Speaker1: That makes sense. Walking can be my backup plan when I don’t feel like swimming. It is important for me to balance my study pressure with physical activity, and I’m glad there are so many free campus choices available.

Speaker2: Exactly. Expensive club memberships are not necessary for us to stay active. We can take good advantage of campus resources. Maybe we can try some of these activities together sometime.

Speaker3: I’d like that. Whether we take a walk in green spaces or get an affordable drink later, these low-cost activities can keep us physically and mentally refreshed through the whole semester. Additionally, I also enjoy cooking in the kitchen, as preparing simple meals helps me take a break from academic work and restore energy for the next day's study. These activities help me manage the workload without adding financial stress.
```
