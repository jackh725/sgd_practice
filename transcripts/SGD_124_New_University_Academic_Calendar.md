## 音频原文 (Transcript)

```text
Narration: three students are discussing the university’s new academic calendar policy.

Speaker1: Have you heard? The school might introduce a new school calendar. The academic calendar has been changed, and summer is now being used for compulsory classes. There will be two timetables, one for the two-semester academic year, the other for the four-semester academic year.

Speaker2: Yeah, I heard about it too. Honestly, I’m a bit worried because I already arranged an internship for the summer, and other people have probably made plans as well. A lot of students rely on the summer break to gain work experience.

Speaker3: Exactly. Many internships can only be arranged during the summer, so this policy could make us miss out on valuable internship opportunities. Employers usually expect students to be available during that period.

Speaker1: I haven’t planned anything myself yet, but I was hoping for a more flexible summer. I would like being able to travel, relax a little, or spend time with family before the next semester starts.

Speaker2: Anyway, If summer classes are not compulsory, students may lack self-discipline at home and end up wasting time. From what I know, the school is only asking for feedback at the moment. The policy probably won’t be implemented until a few years later. If it really happens, though, we’d probably end up having nine breaks throughout the year instead of one long summer vacation.

Speaker3: That’s exactly the biggest downside. A long summer break gives us a complete block of free time to develop ourselves, whether it’s doing internships, learning new skills or taking a good rest. Those nine short breaks scattered across the year are way less useful. They’re too short for internships or long-term study plans, and we can’t even travel far or arrange systematic personal development.

Speaker1: I totally agree with you. Short fragmented breaks can’t replace a full summer vacation at all. Besides, switching between study and break so frequently might make us feel tired and unable to focus on our coursework. It will also mess up our personal schedules and long-term study plans.

Speaker2: Right. I also think the new four-semester model is a bit unreasonable. The original two-semester system is stable and suitable for most students’ study rhythm. Compulsory summer classes will greatly reduce our spare time and increase our academic pressure invisibly.

Speaker3: Hopefully the school will take all students’ feedback seriously. Most of us have made fixed summer plans for study, work and life. Blindly implementing this new policy will bring huge inconvenience to every student. It’s better for the school to optimize the current calendar instead of making drastic changes.
```
